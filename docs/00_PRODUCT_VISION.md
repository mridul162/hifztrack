# Product vision

## Purpose

HifzTrack helps people preserve and improve Quran memorization through personal progress records, structured revision, and supportive groups. It should make progress visible while keeping personal data under the learner's control.

Hifz communities often rely on spreadsheets, messaging groups, or manual records. This makes it hard to centralize a learner's progress, monitor many members, distinguish new memorization from revision, understand group progress, sustain accountability, and verify learning consistently through mentors. HifzTrack provides one personal Hifz record that learners can selectively share with multiple communities and institutions.

## Audience

- Individual learners tracking memorization and Muraja'ah.
- Peer groups and study circles coordinating progress.
- Mentors supporting learners and optionally verifying assessments.
- Institutions that may later manage multiple groups.

Informal communities are the primary MVP audience: friends, university circles, families, and online accountability groups. Madrasas, Quran learning centers, Hifz institutions, and teacher-led programs are supported by the architecture/data model; complex institutional administration is not MVP scope.

## Product principles

1. A learner owns their memorization history; groups receive only the views the learner permits.
2. Quran references use a canonical source and stable Surah/Ayah identifiers.
3. Memorization and revision are distinct activities.
4. Progress should be understandable, deterministic, and auditable.
5. Mentorship and verification can support learning but do not define ownership or completion.
6. The product vision includes future capabilities; delivery scope is controlled separately in the roadmap.

## Desired outcome

Learners can reliably record what they memorized and revised, understand their progress, and share an appropriate level of information with trusted groups. Groups can encourage consistency without bypassing each learner's privacy settings.

## Scope boundary

The product is planned as a responsive web application. Audio, offline synchronization, integrations, and AI evaluation are future capabilities, not MVP commitments. Their details require product decisions before implementation.
